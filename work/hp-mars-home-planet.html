The framing

HP came to Local Motors wanting a community design challenge to showcase their high-end workstations. They knew it should involve Mars, but not much beyond that. At the time, the public conversation was already about getting people to Mars and setting up a first colony. Rather than follow that, we pushed further out. What would a city of a million people on Mars look like, built by people who saw it as home?

I ran the initial discovery workshop, set the scope of the concept, and named it Mars: Home Planet. The name did two jobs. It described a future where Mars was home, not an outpost, and it carried HP's initials. Pushing the horizon out gave the community room to design a whole society instead of a single habitat. We broke it into six challenges, from transportation and farming to lifestyle and urbanization, and worked alongside NASA's planetary science team, including Chris McKay and Pascal Lee, to keep the ideas grounded. Thousands of entries came in from tens of thousands of participants. HP made the resulting VR experience the centerpiece of Autodesk University and extended the project internationally.
