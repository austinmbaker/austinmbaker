The reframe

The original brief was for a Tactical Mobility Vehicle. But when we went to 29 Palms and spent time with the Marines who would actually use it, the real problems were logistics on base, not mobility in the field. The name mattered too. "Tactical" suggested combat, and that isn't what this work was for. So we changed the brief to a Modular Logistics Vehicle, a name that described the actual problem and set honest expectations about how the product would be used.

That one change reshaped everything downstream. Instead of a single-purpose vehicle, the community designed a platform. The result was HAT 2.0, a modular electric base with four removable box configurations, taken all the way to a full-scale prototype. The Marine Corps considered it a clear success, and it opened new innovation partnerships with Local Motors. The lesson I keep coming back to is simple. A brief is a hypothesis. Talk to the people who'll use the product before you treat it as fact.
